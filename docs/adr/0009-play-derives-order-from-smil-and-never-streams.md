# `play` derives the order from SMIL, plays local files, and never streams

`dod play` builds a Playlist from the book's own structure and plays only files that are already on disk. For DAISY 2.02 the order comes from `master.smil` if present, otherwise from the SMIL files in the order `ncc.html` first references them; for EPUB 3 it comes from the spine and each item's `media-overlay`. In both cases every `<audio src clip-begin clip-end>` is collected in document order, nested `seq` included, into Clips. Headings, page numbers and text are ignored, which is what keeps this short of full DAISY navigation (out of scope). There is no fallback to file-name order or to the order of the `resources` answer, since the specification guarantees neither. A book with no usable SMIL (`audioOnly`, an EPUB without media overlays) fails with an explanation. SMIL, OPF and `ncc.html` are untrusted input as in ADR 0008: they are parsed safely with `lxml` and every path is validated like a `localURI`.

The DODP v1 specification treats downloading and streaming as Reading System behaviour, and only requires a Service (not a Service Delegate) to honour HTTP Range requests (section 3.1.4). We therefore do not stream. By default `play` refuses files that are missing; with `--fetch-missing` it fetches missing audio files one at a time through `fetch_resource` just before they are played, under the rules of ADR 0008. Without that flag nothing is fetched, so `play` never hides the not-issued case.

`dod.domain.book` is a pure package, innermost beside `dod.domain.service` and `dod.domain.v1`, that turns bytes into a Playlist or a typed error and knows nothing about DODP. Playback is an adapter around `miniaudio` behind a `Player` seam, wired to the parser by the `play` use case. `miniaudio` decodes WAV, FLAC, MP3 and Ogg Vorbis but has no pause, seek or position on a running device. Pause, resume and skip of 30 seconds are therefore a stop followed by a restart from a frame offset, and the position is our own count of frames handed to the device, which runs slightly ahead of what has been heard. We declare only `audio/mpeg` and `audio/wav` in `setReadingSystemAttributes`; a resource in another format fails to play and is reported as an Observation. While playing, the display shows the audio file's path within the book (the `localURI`, or the manifest `href` in EPUB), its index among the distinct files of the Playlist ("3 of 7") and the elapsed Playlist time.

We rejected the order of the `resources` answer and file-name sorting (neither is guaranteed), streaming (Range is not required of Service Delegates, and it would carry the Session cookie towards the audio layer), implicit fetching (it would make not-issued content untestable, as in ADR 0008) and an ffmpeg dependency for AAC (the licence and packaging cost is not justified for a test client).
